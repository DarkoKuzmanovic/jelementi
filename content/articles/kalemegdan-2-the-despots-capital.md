Near the middle of the Upper Town there is a scale model of a castle, the work of the sculptor Kolja Milunović. It stands roughly in the centre of the castle it shows, which is no longer there. Late in the seventeenth century, during a Turkish siege, a huge explosion of gunpowder almost completely destroyed the castle's ramparts and towers. Clearing the ruins left a mostly flat space, quite unlike the original, and that is the ground visitors walk on today.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en)

The castle belonged to Despot Stefan Lazarević, who made Belgrade the capital of Serbia for the first time. The fortress's official history opens with a line attributed to him: "...I found the most beautiful place since ancient times, the big city of Belgrade..."[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) This part is about how the city came to him, what he built there, and how Serbia lost it by treaty and Hungary lost it by siege.

## A frontier town between empires

[Part 1](https://jelementi.quz.ma/articles/kalemegdan-1-the-white-ridge) ended in 878, with Belgrade's first appearance in writing. For the next four centuries it was a border town, and its owners changed often. The Byzantines held it in the eleventh and twelfth centuries, and in those unsettled times it was demolished and rebuilt several times.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

Crusaders passed through in 1096 and 1147, and in 1189 Belgrade was the meeting place of the army of the Third Crusade, led by the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

The Byzantine emperor Manuel I Komnenos, who ruled from 1143 to 1180, re-established the empire's border on the Danube and rebuilt the city's fortifications. His builders put up towers and ramparts in the Byzantine manner and, in the Upper Town, a castle shaped like a kite, about 135 metres long and 60 metres wide.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) This small fort, or castellum, was the core of the later castle, and a stretch of its rampart, with two towers, can still be seen at the edge of the slope behind the present rampart.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en)

Through the thirteenth century Belgrade was in Hungarian hands, with only minor interruptions.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

## The first Serbian Belgrade

The first Serbian ruler of Belgrade got it by way of an abdication and a marriage. At an assembly at Deževo in 1282, King Dragutin gave up the Serbian throne in favour of his younger brother, Milutin, and kept part of the country for himself. Dragutin's wife, Katarina, was a daughter of the Hungarian king Stephen V, and in the middle of 1284 he took control of the region of Mačva together with Belgrade.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) The City of Belgrade's timeline counts this as the first time Belgrade was under Serbian rule.[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)

Records of Dragutin's Belgrade are scarce, but one visit is reliably known.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) Queen Simonida, Milutin's young wife and a Byzantine princess, came most probably in 1315. She bowed before an icon of the Holy Virgin that was believed to work miracles and had been the town's greatest relic for well over a century.[[4]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%99%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B0-%D1%83-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B8-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%83/?lang=en)[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

The icon was kept in the city's cathedral, the Church of the Assumption of the Mother of God. Nobody knows exactly when it was built. Archbishop Danilo II describes it in Dragutin's time as a large cathedral church of the metropolitan, the city's senior bishop, and it was probably a basilica with three aisles and a large dome.[[5]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B3-%D0%B4%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B0/?lang=en)

Serbian Belgrade did not last. Dragutin died in 1316. In March that year Milutin occupied his brother's lands, and he held them until 1319, when a Hungarian offensive took Belgrade.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) Later Serbian rulers, Tsar Stefan Dušan and Prince Lazar, fought the Hungarians several times, but Belgrade stayed Hungarian until the beginning of the fifteenth century.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

## The Despot's capital

Stefan Lazarević was the son of Prince Lazar, who was killed at the Battle of Kosovo on 28 June 1389, and of Princess Milica.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en)[[7]](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lazar-Hrebeljanovic) Serbia had been dealt a heavy blow at Kosovo and had to recognise the Turkish sultan as its overlord. As one proof of that obedience, Milica sent her youngest daughter, Olivera, to the sultan's harem.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en)

Stefan kept his vassal's oath until 1402, when Sultan Bayezid was defeated at the Battle of Angora, today's Ankara, and taken captive by the Tatars. Stefan then became a vassal of King Sigismund of Hungary, a Christian ruler strong enough to protect him.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en) In August of the same year, in Constantinople, the Byzantine regent John VII Palaiologos gave him the high title of despot.[[8]](https://www.rastko.rs/knjizevnost/liturgicka/konstantin-zitije_desp_stefana_c.html)

Sigismund gave Stefan Belgrade to govern, and Stefan moved his capital there from Kruševac.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en) Sources give the year as 1403 or 1404.[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) The city became Serbia's military, economic and cultural centre.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

According to his biographer, Constantine the Philosopher, Stefan found the city "destroyed and neglected". He repaired the old fortifications and then built new walls and towers. The city was divided into an Upper and a Lower Town and surrounded by double walls with towers, with a moat on the landward side.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

In the Upper Town, on the site of the Byzantine castle, he built his own castle, with its own wall, towers and moat, entered over a drawbridge.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) A partition wall divided it in two. The court stood in the smaller part, on the ridge. Its most important building had a large hall where the despot received official guests, and there was a chapel, a renowned library and, it is believed, a house for his sister Olivera. The larger part held the court's service buildings and barns.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en) The fortress's history also puts the houses of the nobles and the treasury inside the castle, along with two great towers facing each other, Nebojša and Bojša.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) Nebojša, whose name means "Fearless", was the castle's keep, a great tower with a drawbridge of its own and the court's last line of defence.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en)

The castle's entrance was opposite today's fountain of Sokollu Mehmed Pasha, where parts of the walls of its two gate towers can still be seen. A wooden bridge crossed a defensive trench in front of it.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en)

The Lower Town took its shape in the same years. Its plain and its fortified settlement were laid out at the beginning of the fifteenth century, in the despot's time, and the old western suburb, with a dock for warships, became a purely military quarter. All the most important institutions of the capital were down there: the cathedral, with the residence of the metropolitan of Belgrade in its grounds, and a Catholic church in the middle of the settlement. It was the most densely populated part of the city, with the houses of merchants and craftsmen from Serbia, from Hungarian towns and from the Adriatic coast, above all from Dubrovnik.[[9]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B4%D0%BE%D1%9A%D0%B8-%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4/?lang=en) The City of Belgrade's history adds a hospital and a library to the despot's works and says that traders were given privileges. It also suggests that the city may then have had 40,000 to 50,000 inhabitants.[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)

Stefan found the cathedral run down and had it renovated and enlarged. Constantine the Philosopher describes what it held. Besides the miraculous icon, there were the relics of St Petka and of the Holy Empress Theophano, brought from Bulgaria after the Turks conquered it; icons of the twelve apostles; and a reliquary with the hand of the Holy Emperor Constantine, which is now in the treasury of the Moscow Kremlin.[[5]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B3-%D0%B4%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B0/?lang=en)

Nothing of the church stands today. It was destroyed in the 1720s, and the only piece ever found is part of a marble lintel from its doorway, with the despot's founder's inscription, which turned up by lucky chance in a heap of fallen stone. Beside the church stood a large palace of the metropolitan's court, which excavations since 1985 have dated to the despot's time.[[5]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B3-%D0%B4%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B0/?lang=en) Its ruins, the Remains of the Metropolitan's Court (*Ostaci Mitropolitskog dvora*), are one of the sights of the Lower Town.[[10]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%84%D0%BE/?lang=en)

Stefan was a writer as well as a ruler and a builder. His bronze statue in the Upper Town, 3.2 metres tall and wrapped in a cloak, is by the Belgrade sculptor Nebojša Mitrić and was unveiled in 1982.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en)[[10]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%84%D0%BE/?lang=en)

## The handover

Stefan had no children, and in May 1426 he settled the succession with Sigismund at Tata, a spa town in Hungary. The original agreement is lost and its terms are known from later copies. Stefan asked that, if he died without a legitimate son, his nephew Đurađ Branković should be recognised as heir to the Serbian throne and accepted among the Hungarian nobility. Sigismund agreed, on condition that Serbia renew its vassalage to Hungary and hand back Belgrade, Golubac, Mačva and the lands west of the Drina.[[11]](https://istrazivanja.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/istr/article/download/452/467) The Belgrade clause still puzzles historians, because in January 1427 Stefan was still building the city up as his capital.[[11]](https://istrazivanja.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/istr/article/download/452/467)

He died that summer, suddenly. Constantine the Philosopher tells it plainly. Stefan had set out towards Belgrade. At a place called Glavica he stopped to eat and then went hunting, with a hawk on his hand. He began to lean from one side to the other, as if he were about to fall from his horse. His companions held him up on both sides and led him to his tent, where he said only "Po Đurđa, po Đurđa", meaning "Send for Đurađ". He said nothing more, and the next morning, 19 July 1427, he died.[[8]](https://www.rastko.rs/knjizevnost/liturgicka/konstantin-zitije_desp_stefana_c.html)

A white marble pillar, put up in 1427 by a knight of his retinue, still marks the spot where he fell from his horse, in the village of Markovac near Mladenovac.[[12]](https://mladenovac.rs/srp/znamenitosti/)

Đurađ was recognised as Sigismund's vassal and ruler of Serbia, and then, amid loud protests from the Serbian population, he handed Belgrade over. By November 1427 Sigismund was its undisputed master. He tried to fill the city with Catholic settlers and invited merchants from Dubrovnik with promises of privileges.[[11]](https://istrazivanja.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/istr/article/download/452/467) Serbs had to live in the suburbs and were not allowed into the Upper Town.[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)

Belgrade had been the capital for only 23 years.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en) Đurađ built a new capital at Smederevo, with a new fortress modelled on Belgrade's.[[6]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%83-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%83-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%9B%D1%83/?lang=en)[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)

## Hungarian Belgrade

Faced with more and more frequent Turkish attacks, the Hungarians strengthened and extended the fortress.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) The best-preserved medieval works are on the eastern side of the Upper Town.

The Despot's Gate (*Despotova kapija*), the eastern gate of the Upper Town, was the fortress's main entrance in the Middle Ages. With the north-eastern rampart it is the best-preserved part of the medieval Upper Town, dating from the first half of the fifteenth century, and the fortress describes it as the only gate preserved in its original state. It had a double rampart and two gates, an inner and an outer one. Over the inner gate was a machicolation (*mašikula*), a small defensive balcony projecting from the wall, and below it a niche for an icon of the town's protectress. Beside the gate stands the square Dizdar Tower (*Dizdareva kula*), named after the dizdar, the fortress commander, who lived there in the eighteenth century. Today it houses the observatory of the Ruđer Bošković Astronomical Society.[[13]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BC-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC/?lang=en)

In the middle of the fifteenth century the East Gate got an outer defence, the Zindan Gate (*Zindan kapija*). It is a barbican, a small fortification placed in front of a gate: two massive semicircular towers with a passage between them, and a moat with a wooden drawbridge in front. The towers and the rampart had seven openings for cannon, and this is where the fortress was first defended systematically with firearms. The name came later. *Zindan* is Turkish for a dungeon or prison, and in the eighteenth century the towers probably served as one.[[14]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81-%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%BD-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B5/?lang=en)

The first Turkish attack came in 1440. Sultan Murad II spent months before the walls and failed, although the city was badly damaged.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)[[3]](https://www.beograd.rs/en/discover-belgrade/a2014/History.html)[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/)

## The siege of 1456

Sixteen years later came a much larger attack. In 1456 the 24-year-old sultan Mehmed II, the conqueror of Constantinople, marched on Belgrade, which Hungarian sources call Nándorfehérvár.[[16]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD-%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%B0-%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D0%B4-%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en)[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/)

The siege began on 4 July. A fleet of about 200 boats closed the Danube, and the sultan's artillery began to batter the walls.[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/) Inside, the captain of the city was Mihály Szilágyi. His brother-in-law John Hunyadi, known in Serbian tradition as Sibinjanin Janko, was on his way with a relief army.[[17]](https://historynet.com/ottoman-hungarian-wars-siege-of-belgrade-in-1456/)[[16]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD-%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%B0-%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D0%B4-%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en) With him came crusaders raised by John of Capistrano, a Franciscan preacher whose sermons had recruited thousands of volunteers, many of them peasants.[[17]](https://historynet.com/ottoman-hungarian-wars-siege-of-belgrade-in-1456/)[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/)

On 14 July Hunyadi's boats broke the blockade, and he could bring troops and food into the city.[[17]](https://historynet.com/ottoman-hungarian-wars-siege-of-belgrade-in-1456/) The fortress's history notes that Serbian sailors stood out in this battle on the rivers.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

By then the Ottoman guns had breached the walls in several places. On 21 July Mehmed ordered an all-out assault, which began at sundown and went on all night.[[17]](https://historynet.com/ottoman-hungarian-wars-siege-of-belgrade-in-1456/) The attackers broke into the town, and after midnight the fighting reached the walls of the castle.[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/) The decisive fight was on the wooden bridge in front of the castle entrance, and a memorial plaque near the remains of the drawbridge now marks the spot.[[16]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD-%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%B0-%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D0%B4-%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en) By dawn the Turks had been driven out of the town.[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/)

Hungarian tradition gives that night a hero, Titusz Dugovics (in Serbian, Titus Dugović), who is said to have thrown himself at a Turk planting a flag on the rampart, at the cost of his own life. Modern historians regard him as invented.[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/)

The next day the battle ended in a way nobody had planned. Hunyadi had forbidden any counter-attack, but a few crusaders crossed the Sava and began shooting at the Turks, and more and more joined them. Capistrano crossed the river to call them back and ended up leading them towards the Turkish camp, holding up a cross.[[15]](https://www.hungarianottomanwars.com/1372-1490/1456-the-siege-of-nandorfehervar-belgrade/) Hunyadi's army joined the fighting, the sultan was badly wounded, and under cover of darkness the Ottoman army withdrew, carrying its wounded in 140 wagons.[[17]](https://historynet.com/ottoman-hungarian-wars-siege-of-belgrade-in-1456/)

That evening Capistrano sent the pope a short account of the victory. He dated it from Belgrade, "on the feast of St Mary Magdalene, the very day of the most glorious victory": 22 July 1456.[[18]](https://ams.ceu.edu/1993_4/Andric.pdf) After it, Belgrade was known as the bulwark of Christendom, *Antemurale Christianitatis*.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

"After the great victory, however, great joy did not follow," says the fortress's page on the memorial. On top of hunger and the bombardment came an epidemic.[[16]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD-%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%B0-%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D0%B4-%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en) The historian Stanko Andrić identifies it as dysentery, spread by the masses of unburied bodies. Hunyadi died on 11 August in Zemun, across the Sava, with Capistrano beside him. Capistrano, already ill himself, died on 23 October at the Franciscan friary of Ilok, which he had chosen as the place to die.[[18]](https://ams.ceu.edu/1993_4/Andric.pdf)

## The noon bell

A well-known legend says that church bells ring at noon in memory of this victory. As news of the Ottoman advance reached Rome, Pope Callixtus III issued a call to prayer, published on 19 June 1456 and again on 29 June. Every church was to ring its bells three times a day, in the afternoon between nones and vespers, and everyone who heard them was to say the Lord's Prayer and the Hail Mary three times.[[19]](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27804259W) James Mixson, who translated the document, notes that these lines, together with later commemorations, eventually inspired the legend of a bell rung at noon for the victory.[[19]](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27804259W) The bells were ordered before the battle and are remembered because of it.

## Two cannon towers

After 1456 the defences kept changing to suit gunpowder. Around 1460 a cannon tower was built on the Danube bank to defend the entrance to the Lower Town's port. This is the Nebojša Tower (*Kula Nebojša*): octagonal, originally about 22 metres high, with five or six openings for cannon on each floor.[[20]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%B0-%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%98%D1%88%D0%B0/?lang=en) It did not start with that name. The original Nebojša was the despot's keep up in the castle, and only after that tower was destroyed at the end of the seventeenth century did its name pass to this much smaller tower by the river.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%84%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2/?lang=en)

The Jakšić Tower (*Jakšićeva kula*), built at about the same time on the eastern side of the Lower Town, is the same kind of tower, only smaller, with 15 positions for cannon covering the northern approach. It is named after the Jakšić brothers, medieval nobles known from folklore and epic songs. Archaeology at the fortress practically began with its excavation in 1936, after which the tower was rebuilt, rather freely, on top of its remains.[[21]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%98%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%88%D0%B8%D1%9B%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%B0/?lang=en)

## 1521

The end came with a new sultan. Suleiman the Magnificent began preparing to take Belgrade as soon as he came to the throne, and unlike his predecessors he started by cutting it off from Hungary, taking Šabac, Slankamen and Zemun first. The hardest attack came from across the Sava, by the janissaries, the sultan's elite infantry, under Piri Pasha.[[22]](https://www.mi.sanu.ac.rs/muzej.beograd/d/eng/sad/hron_1.htm) The fall of the Nebojša Tower, whose guns covered the approach to the Lower Town, marked the collapse of the defence.[[20]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%B0-%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%98%D1%88%D0%B0/?lang=en) The Turks broke into the Lower Town, the defenders withdrew to the Upper Town, and after negotiations the city was handed over on 29 August 1521.[[22]](https://www.mi.sanu.ac.rs/muzej.beograd/d/eng/sad/hron_1.htm)

The Hungarian part of the garrison left northwards by boat. The Serbian defenders and townspeople were deported to Constantinople, where their colony, a village called Beli grad and the Beligrad mahala, or quarter, in the city itself, survived until 1907.[[22]](https://www.mi.sanu.ac.rs/muzej.beograd/d/eng/sad/hron_1.htm)

The cathedral where Simonida had prayed became Sultan Suleiman's mosque, and on 30 August, a Friday and the day after the city fell, the sultan performed the Friday prayer.[[9]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B4%D0%BE%D1%9A%D0%B8-%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4/?lang=en) The metropolitan's palace burned.[[5]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B3-%D0%B4%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B0/?lang=en) Belgrade became an important Ottoman stronghold for further campaigns towards the heart of Europe.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) Part 3 picks up the story there.

## Dates to remember

- 1189: the army of the Third Crusade, under Frederick Barbarossa, gathers at Belgrade
- 1284: King Dragutin takes Belgrade, the first Serbian ruler of the city
- 1319: the Hungarians take Belgrade back
- 1402: Stefan Lazarević becomes despot
- 1403 or 1404: Belgrade becomes the capital of Serbia for the first time
- 19 July 1427: Despot Stefan dies; Belgrade returns to Hungary and the capital moves to Smederevo
- 1440: Sultan Murad II besieges Belgrade and fails
- 22 July 1456: John Hunyadi defeats Mehmed II at Belgrade
- around 1460: the Nebojša Tower is built
- 29 August 1521: Suleiman the Magnificent takes Belgrade
