The fortress's own guide calls it the most recognisable symbol of Belgrade: a naked man on a column. The Victor (*Pobednik*) holds a sword in his right hand, a symbol of war, and a falcon in his left, a symbol of peace. He stands on the highest spot of Belgrade Fortress, on top of the ridge above the confluence of the Sava and the Danube, and the column and figure together are 14 metres tall.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA/?lang=en)

He was not meant to be there. The statue began as part of a fountain for Terazije, in the very centre of the city, and it took a war, a change of plan and some objections to his nudity before he reached the ridge.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA/?lang=en) This part follows the fortress from the departure of the Ottoman garrison in 1867, through two world wars, to the park and monument complex of today.

## A church in a powder magazine

[Part 4](https://jelementi.quz.ma/articles/kalemegdan-4-the-keys-of-the-city) ended with the keys. With the Ottoman garrison gone, the fortress lost its importance as a military stronghold.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) It was still an army post, though, and in 1867 the army got a church.

Ružica Church (*Crkva Ružica*), dedicated to the Nativity of the Mother of God, was not built from scratch. To make the military church, an Austrian gunpowder store from the 1730s was adapted, with a bell tower and an altar apse added.[[3]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B6%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0/?lang=en) Popular belief places a medieval church of the same name on the site, but there is no confirmation of that.[[3]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B6%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0/?lang=en)

The name is older than any of the buildings. It comes from the Rosalia, a pagan festival at which the participants were sprinkled with rose petals. The Orthodox feast of the Holy Trinity fell at the same time of year, so many churches built in the Serbian lands in the Middle Ages were popularly known as Ružica, "little rose".[[3]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B6%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0/?lang=en)

## The field becomes a park

The park came next. In 1869 work began on landscaping the city field, the open ground in front of the walls that the Ottomans had called Kalemegdan, from *kale*, fortress, and *megdan*, field. In March 1891 paths were cut and trees planted.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

In 1903 the Small Staircase (*Malo stepenište*), leading down towards Francuska Street, was built to designs by Jelisaveta Načić, the first woman architect in Serbia.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) Today the park has two parts, Great Kalemegdan Park and Little Kalemegdan Park.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

The fountain in the oldest part of the park has a twin. Belgrade's municipal officials commissioned a bronze of a fisherman fighting a snake, the Fisherman (*Ribar*), from the sculptor Simeon Roksandić, who made it during a stay in Rome in 1906. The next year, on its way to the Balkan exhibition in London, the sculpture was lost, and news reached Belgrade that the ship carrying it had sunk. A second casting was made at once, and that is the one in the park. The original eventually turned up, and in 1912 the municipality of Zagreb bought it. It stands in Zagreb's Jesuit Square, so two South Slav capitals have identical fishermen.[[4]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%84%D0%BE%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B0%D1%80/?lang=en)

## The first shells

On 28 July 1914 Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia by telegram.[[5]](https://www.mod.gov.rs/lat/16984/hronologija-prvog-svetskog-rata-u-srbiji16984) At about half past ten that night, Austro-Hungarian tugs towing barges full of soldiers set out from Zemun to force their way into the Sava and land in Belgrade. At half past one in the morning the Serbian defenders blew up the railway bridge across the Sava, and the boats had to turn back. The command monitor *Temes*, an armoured river warship, came out to cover them and opened fire on the barracks in the Lower Town of the fortress. According to the Military Museum, those were the first shells to fall on Belgrade. Three more monitors, *Bodrog*, *Szamos* and *Körös*, joined in.[[6]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/261/recni-monitor-sava-261)

During the war all the old buildings inside the walls were demolished, and the walls themselves were badly damaged.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

Ružica Church was severely damaged in 1915.[[3]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B6%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0/?lang=en) So was the Dizdar Tower (*Dizdareva kula*) beside the Despot's Gate, named after the dizdar, the fortress commander, who lived in it in the second half of the eighteenth century.[[7]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BC-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC/) The first building of the Military Museum, founded in 1878, was destroyed.[[8]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/230/o-vojnom-muzeju-230)

A monument to Karađorđe, the leader of the First Serbian Uprising, had been unveiled in the park in 1913, to honour the successful end of the Balkan Wars, with King Petar I present. Only two years later, Austrian grenades destroyed it completely.[[9]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%85%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8-%D1%84%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%86%D1%83%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D1%98/?lang=en)

On 6 October 1915 a new attack on Serbia began, this time by combined German and Austro-Hungarian forces, under the German general August von Mackensen. Bulgaria entered the war on their side on 14 October, and Serbia was attacked from the east and the south as well.[[5]](https://www.mod.gov.rs/lat/16984/hronologija-prvog-svetskog-rata-u-srbiji16984)[[10]](https://www.britannica.com/place/Serbia/The-Ten-Years-War) On 25 November the government ordered the army to retreat across Montenegro and Albania.[[5]](https://www.mod.gov.rs/lat/16984/hronologija-prvog-svetskog-rata-u-srbiji16984) The retreat went over the mountains in winter, to the Adriatic coast, and French ships carried what was left of the army to the Greek island of Corfu.[[10]](https://www.britannica.com/place/Serbia/The-Ten-Years-War) The Serbian army came back to Belgrade three years later, on 1 November 1918, and on 1 December the new Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes was proclaimed in the city.[[5]](https://www.mod.gov.rs/lat/16984/hronologija-prvog-svetskog-rata-u-srbiji16984)[[10]](https://www.britannica.com/place/Serbia/The-Ten-Years-War)

## The Victor

The statue that now stands on the ridge had been made before the war. The monument to the Serbian army's victory in the Balkan Wars was first conceived as a fountain for Terazije, and the work was entrusted to Ivan Meštrović, one of the most important artists of the early twentieth century. By the start of the First World War he had cast its central part, the naked man with the sword and the falcon.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA/?lang=en)

When the war began, the figure was stored in a house in Senjak, and it came through the bombing undamaged. After the war the fountain idea was dropped, and the Victor went on top of a high pedestal in the form of a Doric column. The site in the city centre was given up as well. In the fortress's words, "some people reacted badly to the idea of a naked man being in a central location", so a new place was chosen far from the centre.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA/?lang=en)

That place was the highest point of the fortress, on top of the ridge above the confluence. The statue faces the Sava and, across it, lands that had just been liberated and joined to the new South Slav kingdom, and it stands for victory in the First World War. Under a new name, "The Harbinger of Victory", it was unveiled in 1928, during the celebrations of the tenth anniversary of the breakthrough on the Thessaloniki front (*Solunski front*). It was declared a cultural heritage property in 1992, and it was fully restored at the end of 2019 and the beginning of 2020.[[1]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA/?lang=en)

## Between the wars

The garrison church was rebuilt first. Ružica was thoroughly renovated in 1925 to a design by the Russian architect Nikolay Krasnov, and it got its present look. As a military church, it has massive chandeliers made from trophy weapons of the First World War. The two bronze soldiers by the entrance, a medieval warrior of Emperor Dušan's time and a Serbian soldier of the early twentieth century, were cast from cannon shells. The Russian painter Andrei Bitsenko painted the frescoes in 1938. The vault shows a procession of Serbian rulers, and in the Sermon on the Mount of Olives on the west wall he included contemporaries such as Tsar Nicholas II and Kings Petar and Aleksandar Karađorđević.[[3]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B6%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0/?lang=en)

The park got its present appearance between the two world wars.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) In 1928 the architect Aleksandar Krstić designed the Grand Staircase (*Veliko stepenište*), part of the Sava promenade that leads up to the King's Gate and the new Victor. The first archaeological excavations of the fortress began around the same time, and they continue to this day.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en)

Meštrović made a second monument for the park, the Monument of Gratitude to France (*Spomenik zahvalnosti Francuskoj*). It stands where the Karađorđe monument had been, in a part of the park laid out like a French garden, with tall, finely shaped conifers and flower beds. It was raised on the initiative of the Society of Friends of France and the Society of Former Students of French Schools. At the top, a bronze woman with a sword stands for France rushing to help Serbia. Reliefs on the sides of the pedestal show military cooperation between the two countries and French help in educating Serbian young people, and the inscription on the back reads: "We love France as it loved us in 1914–1918". The monument was unveiled in October 1930, with King Alexander I present. It became a cultural monument in 1965 and a cultural monument of great importance in 1983.[[9]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA-%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%85%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8-%D1%84%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%86%D1%83%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D1%98/?lang=en)

Little Kalemegdan got a gallery. The Cvijeta Zuzorić Art Pavilion (*Umetnički paviljon Cvijeta Zuzorić*) was built there in 1928 with money from the Cvijeta Zuzorić Society of Art Lovers. The playwright Branislav Nušić started the project, with the idea of "spreading interest for art", and the architect Branislav Kojić designed the building.[[11]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BF%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%99%D0%BE%D0%BD-%D1%86%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B7%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%9B/?lang=en) It was the first gallery space in the region built solely for art events. From its first year it held two large shows, the Spring Exhibition of Yugoslav artists and the Autumn Exhibition of Belgrade artists, and the tradition continues. Since 1945 the pavilion has been run by the Association of Fine Artists of Serbia (ULUS), which has its headquarters there.[[12]](https://ulus.rs/cvijeta-zuzoric/?lang=en)[[11]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%BF%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%99%D0%BE%D0%BD-%D1%86%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B7%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%9B/?lang=en)

The Military Museum came back as well. In 1934 King Alexander I issued a decree establishing the Military Museum of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, and in 1937 a new exhibition opened in two buildings in the fortress. One of them now houses the city's Institute for the Protection of Cultural Monuments.[[8]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/230/o-vojnom-muzeju-230)

The animals arrived in 1936. The Belgrade Zoo was founded that year by the mayor, the industrialist Vlada Ilić, and it opened on St Peter's Day, 12 July. Its first residents included lions, leopards, white and brown bears, wolves, monkeys, antelopes, pelicans and parrots. It quickly became one of Belgraders' favourite places, and the royal family visited often. It started on just over three and a half hectares and grew to more than fourteen, taking in part of the Lower Town.[[13]](https://www.beozoovrt.rs/istorijat/)

Near Ružica, St Petka's Chapel (*Kapela Svete Petke*) was built in 1937 to a design by the architect Momir Korunović, in the style of Serbian medieval churches. It stands over a spring that has been considered miraculous since the Middle Ages. Nobody knows what the original chapel looked like; only the vaulted space above the spring survives. The first frescoes quickly decayed, so the whole interior was covered with mosaics instead. When the foundations were dug, the workers found the bones of Serbian soldiers who had died defending Belgrade in 1914 and 1915.[[14]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en)

The saint's relics had been in Belgrade before. Through the efforts of Princess Milica at the end of the fourteenth century, they came first to Kruševac and later to Belgrade, the new Serbian capital. After the conquest of 1521, Sultan Suleiman ordered much of the city's population to move to Constantinople, and they took the relics with them. Today they are in Iași, in Romania. The Serbian Orthodox Church celebrates St Petka's day on 27 October.[[14]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en)

## The second war

The Second World War brought the bombers back. The zoo was bombed twice, in 1941 by the Germans and in 1944 by the Allies, and almost all its animals were killed.[[13]](https://www.beozoovrt.rs/istorijat/)

The zoo's history page also records the memory of Dana Savković-Gligorić, whose father, Miodrag Savković, was the zoo's director at the time. During the 1941 bombing, she recalled, people from the surrounding buildings ran into the zoo for shelter, believing it would not be a target. The bombs fell indiscriminately, and many people were killed, especially in the cave that now shelters the zebras and antelopes. Many animals escaped from wrecked cages, and those that were a danger to people had to be killed. Among the few that survived were a hippopotamus called Buca and an alligator called Muja.[[13]](https://www.beozoovrt.rs/istorijat/)

In the Anglo-American bombing of 1944, the boarding house next to St Petka's Chapel was destroyed.[[14]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D1%86%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B5/?lang=en) The Military Museum's old buildings were badly damaged too.[[8]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/230/o-vojnom-muzeju-230) After the war the zoo shrank to about seven hectares, roughly its size today. It calls itself the Garden of Good Hope (*Vrt dobre nade*).[[13]](https://www.beozoovrt.rs/istorijat/)

## Under protection

In 1946 Belgrade Fortress and Kalemegdan were placed under state protection.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) In 1959 the Military Museum moved into its present building, on Bastion 1 of the fortress's south-eastern front. The building went up between 1924 and 1928 for the Military Geographical Institute, to a design by Vasiliev, a Russian émigré architect, and the architect Đuka Kavurić adapted it for the museum between 1956 and 1959.[[8]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/230/o-vojnom-muzeju-230)

The museum also has an exhibit on the river. *Bodrog*, one of the monitors that shelled Belgrade in 1914, kept firing in support of the attacks on the city through 1914 and 1915. On 31 October 1918, sailing upstream in fog, it ran aground near Višnjica, and the Serbs captured it. In 1920 it joined the flotilla of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes under a new name, *Sava*. On the night of 11 to 12 April 1941 its own crew sank it near the confluence, so that it would not fall into German hands. It was raised and sunk once more during the war, and afterwards it was raised again and served in the Yugoslav river flotilla until 1959. In October 2015 the Ministry of Defence bought the ship and handed it over to the Military Museum. The river monitor *Sava* is now a museum ship, moored at the Sava pier on the right bank, downstream from Branko's Bridge.[[6]](https://www.muzej.mod.gov.rs/eng/261/recni-monitor-sava-261)

The Dizdar Tower was partly rebuilt in 1938, and at the end of the 1970s its crenellated top was reconstructed. Today it houses the observatory of the Ruđer Bošković Astronomical Society.[[7]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B4%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BC-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC/)

Together, the fortress and the park are now a cultural monument of exceptional importance.[[2]](https://www.beogradskatvrdjava.co.rs/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0-%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%92%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5/?lang=en) A frontier fortress has become a monument park, with a gallery, a zoo, an observatory and a war museum in and around its walls. Part six walks through it, gate by gate.

## Dates to remember

- 1867: Ružica Church, a former Austrian gunpowder store
- 1869: work begins on Kalemegdan Park
- 1903: the Small Staircase, by Jelisaveta Načić, Serbia's first woman architect
- 28 July 1914: Austria-Hungary declares war; that night the first shells hit the barracks in the Lower Town
- 1915: bombardment damages Ružica and the Dizdar Tower; in October Mackensen's offensive drives the Serbian army into retreat across Albania
- 1918: the Serbian army returns to Belgrade on 1 November, and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes is proclaimed there on 1 December
- 1928: the Victor, the Grand Staircase and the Cvijeta Zuzorić pavilion
- October 1930: the Monument of Gratitude to France
- 1936: the Belgrade Zoo opens on 12 July
- 1937: St Petka's Chapel, by Momir Korunović
- 1941 and 1944: bombing wrecks the zoo
- 1946: the fortress and Kalemegdan come under state protection
- 1959: the Military Museum moves into its present building
