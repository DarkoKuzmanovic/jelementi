The Economy Museum in Stockholm has a sheet of copper that weighs 19.7 kilograms.[[1]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d1cb7528-6fbb-4558-9599-1670cafabadc) A plate of this kind measures about 33 by 62 centimetres.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Like other Swedish plates, it was stamped in the corners and the centre and left blank on the back.[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) It could pass for a tray.

It is a coin. Its value was ten daler in silver money, and the museum calls it the largest coin in the world.[[1]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d1cb7528-6fbb-4558-9599-1670cafabadc) The mint at Avesta, in the Swedish province of Dalarna, produced 26,774 of these plates in 1644 and 1645. Eight survive.[[1]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d1cb7528-6fbb-4558-9599-1670cafabadc)

It was not small change. You would think twice before taking a coin like this to market. The Swedish solution, worked out over the following two decades, was to leave the copper in a bank and carry paper instead. That bank went on to issue the first banknotes in Europe, and then collapsed.[[4]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=18803b0a-f5d5-43cc-b8c3-0b3ca02520a9)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/)

## A country rich in the wrong metal

Sweden in the early 1600s was short of silver. After the Kalmar War of 1611 to 1613, it had to pay Denmark a large ransom to recover the fortress of Älvsborg, and that payment left the country short of coins.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) Silver was scarce. Copper was not.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)

In the first half of the seventeenth century, the great mine at Falun alone supplied about two-thirds of Europe's copper.[[2]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=221a612d-06ff-4fb2-8e42-5fc36f8aa691)[[7]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/copper-standard-is-introduced/)[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) The government saw a way to make that work twice. In 1624 Sweden moved to a copper standard and began minting copper coins.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[7]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/copper-standard-is-introduced/) More coins would ease the shortage. And copper turned into coins could not be exported, so less copper would reach the international metal market in Amsterdam and the price of Sweden's most important export should rise.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[7]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/copper-standard-is-introduced/) The state set up a copper monopoly to control exports. Nobles, burghers and the church were encouraged to put copper on their roofs.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The first copper coins were small and square. They were easy to trim at the edges without anyone noticing, so after a few years they were replaced with round ones.[[7]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/copper-standard-is-introduced/)

The deeper problem was that in those days a coin was expected to contain metal worth roughly its face value.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Copper was cheap compared with silver. According to the Riksbank's own history, citing the economic historian Eli Heckscher, a sum paid in copper coins weighed almost a hundred times as much as the same sum in silver.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) A skeppund of copper, about 136 kilograms, was worth on average 50 riksdaler in the seventeenth century; 50 riksdaler in silver coins weighed less than a kilogram and a half.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

Heckscher also recorded two thieves in Åbo, the Finnish city now called Turku, who tried to steal 170 daler in copper coins. They could not lift it above their knees.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) When Queen Christina's government considered more copper coinage in 1649, one of the strongest objections was the cost of moving tax revenue around the country.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The arrangement had other faults. If the price of copper rose high enough, the metal in a coin was worth more than its face value, and it paid to ship the coins abroad and sell them. Bigger, heavier coins were all the easier to sell as copper bars.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Silver coins were also still in use, and their value against copper moved about. In effect Sweden ran three currencies at once: the riksdaler for foreign trade, the daler in silver money and the daler in copper money.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Merchants had to keep track of all three.

## Money you could sell by weight

The plates came next. In 1644, under Queen Christina, Sweden struck its first copper plate money.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) The Riksbank's history describes this first issue as a trial, and calls it a mistake: the government had stabilised the copper coinage the year before and should have kept to small change.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) In 1648 the industrialist Louis De Geer pressed for plate money to be minted regularly. The chancellor Axel Oxenstierna had opposed copper coinage, but now agreed to try it. He and De Geer argued that plates suited international trade because they could be "used both as money and as merchandise."[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The logic was simple. A plate's value matched the weight of its copper, so a buyer abroad could treat it as either coin or raw metal.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) Plates were hammered into sheets, cut to size and stamped: the ruler's name or initials and the year in the corners, the value in the middle.[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) Denominations soon ran from ten daler down to half a daler.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) Avesta made most of them, but there were also mints in Stockholm, in the mountains of Härjedalen and Jämtland, and at Kengis in the far north.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) The museum describes Kengis as the northernmost mint in the world, and notes that many Sámi people worked there.[[2]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=221a612d-06ff-4fb2-8e42-5fc36f8aa691)

Even the smaller plates were heavy, and paying with them took effort. Swedes needed somewhere to put them.

## A bank to hold the weight

The man who supplied it was Johan Palmstruch. He was born Hans Witmacker in Riga in 1611, the son of a Dutch merchant.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) In his twenties he moved to Amsterdam and became a burgher of the city. In 1639 he was arrested for unpaid debts and spent some years in prison before reaching an agreement with his creditors in 1643.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) His family was ennobled in 1651 and took the name Palmstruch.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

Amsterdam had a famous exchange bank, and Palmstruch came home with ideas about banking.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product) His early proposals in Stockholm went nowhere. Then, on 30 November 1656, during a war with Poland, King Karl X Gustav signed charters at his military headquarters in Marienburg allowing Palmstruch and his partners to open an exchange bank and a loan bank.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Stockholms Banco was Sweden's first bank. It was privately owned on paper, but half its profit went to the Crown and the king appointed its supervisors, so in practice it was a state institution.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product)

The king's charter was frank about the bank's purpose. Depositing copper would spare his subjects "much subtraction and addition, hauling and dragging and other trouble that the copper coin entails in its handling."[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The bank opened on 29 July 1657. Its first depositor, the councillor Seved Bååt, brought in 13,763 daler in copper money.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) By the spring of 1660, deposits had passed 400,000 daler.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Each kind of coin had its own column in the books and its own place in the vaults, and depositors were to be repaid in the same kind of coin they had brought.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) That rule was about to matter a great deal.

Unlike the exchange bank in Amsterdam, it also started lending out its depositors' money.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Palmstruch later testified that the supervisor, Gustaf Bonde, came into the exchange bank one morning in 1659, looked around, and said: "I see here in the exchange bank good stores of money and it seems to me to be best now to make a beginning with the loan bank."[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Borrowers arrived in such numbers that, by Palmstruch's account, guards had to be posted to keep them from the doors.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Most loans went to the nobility. Ebba Brahe, once the sweetheart of King Gustav II Adolf, borrowed 2,700 daler against a silver candelabra.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

## The day the copper got lighter

In February 1660 Karl X Gustav died, and the Council of State began governing on behalf of the child king. In March and again in November it issued ordinances that changed the value of copper money.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) When plates were introduced in 1644, a skeppund of copper had been reckoned at 69 daler in silver money. By 1649 the rate was 75. The 1660 ordinances raised it to 84 and then to 90.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) New plates of a given face value would now contain less copper than old ones.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/)

That meant the old plates were worth more than their face value as metal. Depositors came to Stockholms Banco asking for the same kind of plate they had handed over, as the charter entitled them to.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/) But the bank had lent much of that copper out. It started calling in loans and cut back new lending. It bought copper to have new plates struck at Avesta, but that took too long.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Lending was cut back just as demand for loans peaked. Palmstruch wrote to the Council that people besieged him at the bank, and even at home "morning, afternoon and evening," asking to borrow.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

## Paper with nothing behind it but a promise

Palmstruch's answer was a piece of paper. The bank would issue notes saying that the holder could claim a fixed sum from Stockholms Banco, and the bank would pay it in coin on request.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) He later said the idea came from Stora Kopparberg, the company that ran the Falun mine. When miners weighed in their copper they received receipts, and those receipts passed from hand to hand in the community as money.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

Paper claims were not new in themselves. Receipts for deposits had been passed between merchants before, and in Sweden mining companies and private people had used note-like documents.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) China had issued paper money in the eleventh century.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) What was new in Europe was that Palmstruch's notes were not tied to any particular deposit.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/) A note came in a round amount and named no depositor.[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product) It was worth something because people trusted the bank to pay it.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/)

The first notes went out in July or August 1661.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) None of that first issue survives; the best-known surviving notes date from 1666.[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product) They were printed on thick, white handmade paper watermarked with the word BANCO.[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product) Parts were filled in by hand, including the number and the last digit of the date.[[10]](https://museum.nbb.be/en/resources/first-notes-european-bank-swedish-product) One ten-daler note in the Museum of Gothenburg reads, in translation, that the bearer has the right to collect ten daler from the bank. It carries eight signatures and ten seals, belonging to Palmstruch, the bank's commissioners, its bookkeeper and its cashiers.[[11]](https://goteborgsstadsmuseum.se/en/online/vardefullt/history-of-money/europes-first-banknote/the-palmstruch-banknote/)

People liked them. A sheet of paper about twenty centimetres across was easier to carry than a slab of copper.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/)[[11]](https://goteborgsstadsmuseum.se/en/online/vardefullt/history-of-money/europes-first-banknote/the-palmstruch-banknote/) According to Palmstruch, notes were "asked for with such an appetite and cry" that the bank sometimes struggled to keep up, and the bank's agent in Gothenburg reported that people happily exchanged coins for them.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) Palmstruch also claimed they were accepted in Riga, Danzig, Lübeck, Hamburg, Amsterdam, London, Paris and Venice.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) He was not a neutral witness.

## Printing your way out of trouble

The notes solved one problem and made a bigger one possible. Lending no longer depended on deposits. The bank could lend as much as it was willing to print.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/)

In 1663 it printed a great deal. The Crown borrowed 500,000 daler in copper money. The chancellor, Magnus Gabriel De la Gardie, borrowed 255,000 for himself. The tar company took 200,000.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) More and more loans were made without security, and new branches opened in Åbo, Falun and Gothenburg.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

On 12 September 1663 a bank secretary wrote to Palmstruch, who was away in Västerås, that so much had been withdrawn that the bank had less than 4,000 daler in cash. One depositor had just announced that he wanted to take out 10,000. The secretary asked Palmstruch to send money. Palmstruch had nothing to send.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

As notes grew harder to redeem, they lost value. Paying with notes cost 6 to 10 percent more than paying in coin, and prices rose.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) One contemporary complaint put it plainly: "Everything one buys must now be bought for more than before, so that everyone suffers unduly."[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) The Riksbank's history calls this Sweden's first bout of monetary inflation.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) The total of notes was not huge, about 2.7 million daler in copper money at the end of 1664. What the market could not absorb was how fast the amount had grown, in little more than a year.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

At the parliament of 1664, the clergy described what redeeming a note was like: "In the evening they carry money away from [the Bank] and elsewhere and when one goes to the Bank and wants money, they turn you away and say 'Go there or there, you'll get it' and when you get there, you have to give 8 or 10 per cent."[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The government ordered an inquiry in October 1663. De la Gardie, who proposed it, was also the bank's largest borrower.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) The Council eventually decided that loans should be called in and the notes withdrawn.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[8]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe/) That squeezed credit across the country. Borrowers lost estates to private moneylenders, and in 1666 Sweden set its first legal ceiling on interest rates.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

Withdrawing the notes took longer than expected. As fewer remained in circulation, their value recovered until a note was again worth its face value in coin, and people became less keen to cash them in, because notes were convenient.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) The final accounts of the liquidation were presented at the start of 1668.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

## The banker and the bank that replaced him

Palmstruch was tried before the Svea Court of Appeal. He blamed miscalculations, too few staff, impatient customers and the trouble of handling so many kinds of coin. He described messengers from government offices arriving "more than 10 journeys a day, with snork, pork, scolding and swearing," and asked who, amid all that, "could note and thereby keep a book?"[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

The court was not persuaded. On 22 July 1668 it dismissed him, stripped him of his privileges and banished him for life. He was ordered to repay the bank's demonstrable losses within six months. If he could not, he would be executed.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) He could not.[[14]](https://www.bis.org/review/r170627f.htm) The Council considered the sentence in February 1669 and some members found it too harsh. He was reprieved but kept in prison.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf) De la Gardie, the bank's largest borrower, argued for him.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[14]](https://www.bis.org/review/r170627f.htm) Palmstruch was released in March 1670 and died a year later, on 8 March 1671. He was buried in Täby Church, north of Stockholm.[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)[[14]](https://www.bis.org/review/r170627f.htm)

In 1668, the same year he was sentenced, the Riksdag of the Estates founded a new bank from the remains of the old one. It was run on behalf of the nobility, clergy and burghers, and became today's Sveriges Riksbank, the oldest central bank in the world.[[4]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=18803b0a-f5d5-43cc-b8c3-0b3ca02520a9)[[9]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/sveriges-riksbank-is-founded/) At first, the new bank was not allowed to issue banknotes.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[9]](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/sveriges-riksbank-is-founded/)

The ban did not hold well. People missed the notes and used deposit receipts and similar papers instead, which was not allowed, and which the bank found hard to stop.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) In 1701 the Riksbank brought out its own "transport notes." Each person who used one had to sign it, and at first they were backed by actual deposits.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) Not everyone could write their name, so people often tore the signature section off. The requirement was dropped in the end, though the name stayed.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016) From 1726 taxes could be paid with banknotes, and by the eighteenth century they were more common in Sweden than coins.[[4]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=18803b0a-f5d5-43cc-b8c3-0b3ca02520a9)[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)

## The plates' last voyage

The plates outlasted Stockholms Banco by about a century. According to the American Numismatic Association, a decision of November 1766 ordered that plate money should no longer be issued and should be gradually withdrawn, describing it as "almost always unreliable in value with regard to silver, and also expensive as regards costs, apart from being cumbersome and difficult to store."[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) The currency reform of 1776 made the riksdaler Sweden's only main coin and ended the copper standard.[[5]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=3c5a9c6e-3abe-408c-9a47-43ccfe510016)[[6]](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf)

Most of the plates were melted down.[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/) Copper, after all, was still useful as copper. Many of the survivors come from a single shipwreck.[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/)[[13]](https://www.coinworld.com/news/world-coins/swedish-copper-plate-money-from-wreck-in-auction.html)

In the summer of 1783 the Danish East India Company ship *Nicobar* was on its way to India carrying 5,000 Swedish copper plates.[[3]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d9c83d3c-20bc-4dff-99bb-cc377b636d37) By then they were no longer Swedish money. According to the auction house cited by Coin World, they were being sent to be made into coins for Danish India.[[13]](https://www.coinworld.com/news/world-coins/swedish-copper-plate-money-from-wreck-in-auction.html) De Geer had said the plates could be used both as money and as merchandise. These were cargo.

The *Nicobar* sank in a storm off the coast of South Africa in July 1783.[[3]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d9c83d3c-20bc-4dff-99bb-cc377b636d37)[[13]](https://www.coinworld.com/news/world-coins/swedish-copper-plate-money-from-wreck-in-auction.html) Fishermen found the wreck in 1987 and brought up some 3,000 plates.[[12]](https://www.money.org/tales-from-the-vault-swedish-plate-money-too-heavy-to-steal/)[[13]](https://www.coinworld.com/news/world-coins/swedish-copper-plate-money-from-wreck-in-auction.html) Some of them are now on display at the Economy Museum in Stockholm.[[3]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d9c83d3c-20bc-4dff-99bb-cc377b636d37) So is the ten-daler plate from Avesta, 19.7 kilograms of copper that once counted as cash.[[1]](https://pengar.ekonomiskamuseet.se/objects.php?e=no&l=en&showcase=d1cb7528-6fbb-4558-9599-1670cafabadc)
