Most visitors come to Kopaonik in winter, and most of them come to ski. The national operator of Serbia's ski resorts calls it the largest ski resort in the country.[[1]](https://www.skijalistasrbije.rs/en/about-resort-kopaonik) A guide who takes a group there has a chance to show them a different mountain: one that people dug into for its [ore](#glossary-ore) for more than two thousand years, and that a famous scientist loved so much he wanted to stay there for ever.

Kopaonik National Park (*Nacionalni park Kopaonik*) was created by a law the Assembly of the Socialist Republic of Serbia passed on 13 July 1981.[[2]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/about-us/) It protects the high central part of the mountain, chosen for its natural landmarks, its well-preserved natural forests and its cultural and historical heritage.[[3]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/about-park/) Kopaonik itself is a large massif in south-central Serbia, between the Ibar and Sitnica rivers to the west and the valleys of the Rasina and Toplica to the east.[[4]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/natural-features/) The park likes to call it the Mountain of the Sun.[[4]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/natural-features/)

## A summit for Pančić

The best place to start is the top. Kopaonik's highest peak is Pančićev vrh, Pančić's Peak, at 2,017 metres, and in summer as well as winter a [chairlift](#glossary-chairlift) carries visitors up to the view.[[5]](https://www.skijalistasrbije.rs/sr/letnji-sadrzaji)[[1]](https://www.skijalistasrbije.rs/en/about-resort-kopaonik) On the summit stands a small stone [mausoleum](#glossary-mausoleum). It holds the remains of Josif Pančić, the man the peak is named after.[[6]](https://www.sanu.ac.rs/clan/pancic-josif/)

Pančić is the hero of this mountain, and his story is easy to tell. He studied medicine in Pest, where he also attended the lectures of the botanist Josip Sadler.[[6]](https://www.sanu.ac.rs/clan/pancic-josif/) Botany became his life's work, and in 1887 he became the first president of the Serbian Royal Academy.[[6]](https://www.sanu.ac.rs/clan/pancic-josif/) His most famous discovery was the Serbian spruce, which he described in 1875. It grows wild on Tara, not here, but it carries his name.[[6]](https://www.sanu.ac.rs/clan/pancic-josif/)

![A small stone mausoleum with a pitched roof and an inscription plaque on a grassy summit under a blue sky.](articles/national-parks-5-kopaonik/photo-1-v1.jpg "Josif Pančić's mausoleum on Pančićev vrh. Photo: Bobik, CC BY-SA 3.0")

Kopaonik was his favourite mountain. He first came in 1852 and kept coming back for more than thirty years.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/) He wished to rest on Kopaonik for ever, and the Serbian Academy tells how that wish came true much later. In July 1951, Serbian mountaineers carried his remains up to the mausoleum on the highest peak, which has carried his name ever since.[[6]](https://www.sanu.ac.rs/clan/pancic-josif/)

:::fact{title="Sixteen visits"}
Josif Pančić came to Kopaonik sixteen times, from his first visit in 1852 to his last in 1886, when he was seventy-two.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/)
:::

A practical note for guides: access to the summit can change, so check it before promising the group the mausoleum.

## A mountain named after mining

The second story is in the name itself. Kopaonik comes from *kopati*, "to dig", and the park explains that the mountain was named after the mining of ore.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/) People were mining here before the Romans arrived.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/) Under Rome it was part of the mining district of [Dardania](#glossary-dardania), known in Latin as *metalla dardanica*.[[8]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/cultural-values/)

The great age of mining came in the Middle Ages. The park links it to the arrival of the [Saxons](#glossary-saxons), German miners who came to medieval Serbia in the thirteenth century and brought their skills with them.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/) Archaeologists have found the traces of mines and furnaces, mostly along the rivers, where iron and lead were mined and worked.[[9]](https://reff.f.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/5052) Silver mattered most of all, and some peaks still remember it: Srebrnac, above Brzeće, is named after silver (*srebro*).[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/history/)

:::fact{title="Holes in the hillside"}
Walking to Nebeske Stolice, visitors can see well-like holes on the slopes below. The park identifies them as old workings where the Saxons dug for iron ore.[[10]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/hiking-trails/hiking-trail-nebeske-stolice/)
:::

## A church in the clouds

Those holes lie on the way to one of the most atmospheric places on the mountain, Nebeske Stolice, the Heavenly Thrones. On this high ridge stand the foundations of an early Christian church from the fifth or sixth century, dedicated to St Procopius.[[8]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/cultural-values/)

Only the low stone outlines survive, so this is a place where the guide's words do the work. Excavations found fragments of a coloured floor [mosaic](#glossary-mosaic) with geometric patterns and animal figures.[[8]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/cultural-values/) Ask the group to imagine that floor, high on a bare ridge above the forests, in a mining country at the end of the Roman world. The link with mining has lasted. St Procopius is revered as the protector of miners, and people from the surrounding villages still gather here to pray and celebrate his feast.[[10]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/hiking-trails/hiking-trail-nebeske-stolice/)

![Low stone wall foundations on a rocky, grassy ridge, with a small wooden hut and a wooden cross in the background.](articles/national-parks-5-kopaonik/photo-2-v1.jpg "The foundations at Nebeske Stolice. Photo: CrniBombarder!!!, CC BY-SA 3.0")

## From spruce to violet

The ridge is also the place to talk about plants. On the way up the mountain, the forests change with height. Beech and fir give way to spruce, and higher still the spruce thins out into low shrubs of mountain juniper and bilberry, and then high meadows.[[11]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/flora/) A group that walks or rides up can see the whole sequence.

Kopaonik's botanical treasure is small and yellow. The Kopaonik violet (*Viola kopaonikensis*) is a [local endemic](#glossary-local-endemic): it grows nowhere else in the world. On Kopaonik it grows only on [serpentine](#glossary-serpentine) [scree](#glossary-scree), slopes of loose stone, including the ridge at Nebeske Stolice.[[11]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/flora/)[[10]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/hiking-trails/hiking-trail-nebeske-stolice/) Its flowers are yellow, about two centimetres across.[[11]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/flora/) Like the park's other protected plants, it must not be picked, uprooted or damaged.[[11]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/flora/) A photograph is the only souvenir.

:::fact{title="A yellow violet"}
Most people picture violets as purple. Kopaonik's own violet is yellow, and it grows only on this mountain's serpentine scree.[[11]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/flora/)
:::

## Water: a bog, a waterfall and a spring

Kopaonik is full of water. Countless springs feed a dense network of small streams across the mountain.[[4]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/natural-features/) Three places show it well.

Jankove Bare, in the Rečica valley, holds the largest [peat bog](#glossary-peat-bog) on Kopaonik.[[12]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/nature-reserves/jankove-bare/) Its ground is made of peat formed by [sphagnum](#glossary-sphagnum) moss, with spruce growing on it.[[12]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/nature-reserves/jankove-bare/) A wooden boardwalk lets visitors cross without damaging it.

![A wooden boardwalk with railings running across a green meadow towards a dark spruce forest.](articles/national-parks-5-kopaonik/photo-3-v1.jpg "The boardwalk at Jankove Bare. Photo: Andrejnihil, CC BY-SA 4.0")

Jelovarnik is a cascading waterfall on a stream below Pančićev vrh and Nebeske Stolice, surrounded by beech and mixed forest.[[13]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/nature-reserves/jelovarnik/) Different park pages give it different heights, so let the group enjoy it without a number.

![A narrow white waterfall tumbling down a steep rocky gully between green trees.](articles/national-parks-5-kopaonik/photo-4-v1.jpg "The Jelovarnik waterfall. Photo: Andjela1993, CC BY-SA 4.0")

Metođe is both a nature reserve and a place of pilgrimage. Under a rock face about fifteen metres high is a spacious shelter, and in one corner a spring rises that local people believe has healing powers.[[14]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/hiking-trails/hiking-trail-metode/) On the trail, visitors pass an abandoned medieval mine shaft with fresh drinking water flowing from it: the mining story and the water story in one spot.[[14]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/hiking-trails/hiking-trail-metode/) The park also mentions a cold-water "geyser" on the mountain. It is not volcanic. It came out of hydrogeological research.[[4]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/natural-features/)

:::fact{title="The only edelweiss"}
The Gobelja reserve, at the source of the Gobeljska river, is the only place on Kopaonik where edelweiss grows, according to the park.[[15]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/nature-reserves/gobelja/)
:::

## Animals, old and returning

The park's animals include brown trout in its mountain streams and otters along the watercourses.[[16]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/fauna/) One newcomer is an old resident coming back. In 2021 the park brought the first chamois from Tara to Kopaonik, to [reintroduce](#glossary-reintroduction) them to the mountain.[[16]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/fauna/)

The strangest story belongs to a butterfly. The caterpillars of the large blue are carried by ants into their nest. There they feed on the ants' own larvae until they are ready to change into butterflies.[[16]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/fauna/) It is the kind of story a group remembers long after the view.

## Two mountains in one

Kopaonik is where a guide sees most clearly that a national park is not a fence around empty nature. A ski resort, hotels and chairlifts sit beside strict reserves, peat bogs and a fifth-century church.[[1]](https://www.skijalistasrbije.rs/en/about-resort-kopaonik)[[2]](https://npkopaonik.rs/en/about-us/) Winter sport is only one way to know the mountain. For a group in summer, the same chairlift that carries skiers in winter becomes the easiest way to reach Pančić and his view.[[1]](https://www.skijalistasrbije.rs/en/about-resort-kopaonik)

## Dates to remember

- 5th–6th century: the early Christian church at Nebeske Stolice
- 13th century: the Saxons arrive and medieval mining grows
- 1852: Josif Pančić's first visit to Kopaonik
- 1875: Pančić describes the Serbian spruce
- 1886: Pančić's sixteenth and last visit
- 1887: Pančić becomes the first president of the Serbian Royal Academy
- July 1951: mountaineers carry Pančić's remains to the summit mausoleum
- 13 July 1981: the law on Kopaonik National Park is passed
- 2021: the first chamois are brought from Tara
