Ask a group what a national park is and most people picture a fence around empty wilderness: no houses, no roads, nature left alone. Serbia's parks are not like that, and the difference is the best place for a guide to begin.

Under Serbian law a [national park](#glossary-national-park) is an area with many different natural ecosystems of national importance, outstanding landscapes and cultural heritage, where people live in harmony with nature.[[1]](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/srb104543consolidated.pdf) The last part of that sentence matters. Villages, vineyards, monasteries, roads and ski slopes all sit inside Serbian national parks. The law that governs the five parks says they exist to protect three things together: living nature, the rocks and landforms beneath it, and cultural and historical heritage.[[2]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Zakon-o-naconalnim-parkovima.pdf) So a medieval fortress on the Danube or a monastery in the woods of Fruška Gora is as much a part of the park as an eagle or a rare spruce.

Nor are the parks only about keeping people out. The same law lists education, research, sport, recreation and "an impressive experience of nature" for visitors among their purposes.[[2]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Zakon-o-naconalnim-parkovima.pdf) A guide leading a group up a marked trail is doing exactly what a national park is for.

## Five parks, five personalities

The National Parks Act names five: Fruška Gora, Đerdap, Tara, Kopaonik and Šar planina.[[2]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Zakon-o-naconalnim-parkovima.pdf) Each has a character of its own, and a guide can sum up each in a sentence before the group arrives.

**Fruška Gora** (*Nacionalni park Fruška gora*) is the elder of the family. It became a national park in 1960, which makes it the oldest in Serbia.[[3]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/) It is a long wooded mountain in Vojvodina, and its slopes hold not only forest but also numerous monasteries and historical sites.[[3]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/) If anyone in the group doubts that culture belongs in a national park, Fruška Gora settles the argument.

![Vineyards in the foreground and a monastery church with a bell tower behind them, with the flat plain stretching to the horizon.](articles/national-parks-1-what-a-national-park-protects/photo-1-v1.jpg "Vineyards at Grgeteg Monastery on Fruška Gora. Photo: Lukder, CC BY-SA 4.0")

**Đerdap** (*Nacionalni park Đerdap*) is the giant. Its own managers call it the largest national park in Serbia, and it has been protected since 1974.[[4]](https://npdjerdap.rs/o-parku/) It runs along the Serbian bank of the Danube through the great gorge known in English as the Iron Gates. The river is so central to it that Đerdap is also called a river national park.[[4]](https://npdjerdap.rs/o-parku/) Part 3 of this series is devoted to it.

**Tara** (*Nacionalni park Tara*) is the forest park of western Serbia, high above the canyon of the Drina. It was proclaimed a national park on 13 July 1981.[[5]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/biljni-svet.html) Its signature is a tree: the Pančić spruce (*Pančićeva omorika*), which the botanist Josif Pančić found at Sklopovi near the village of Zaovine in 1875.[[5]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/biljni-svet.html)

**Kopaonik** (*Nacionalni park Kopaonik*) is the high one. The law that made it a national park was passed on the very same day as Tara's, 13 July 1981.[[6]](https://npkopaonik.rs/o-nama/) Today most visitors know Kopaonik as Serbia's ski mountain, but the park protects its forests, high pastures and cultural heritage as well.[[7]](https://npkopaonik.rs/o-parku/) Its highest summit, Pančićev vrh, is 2,017 metres high and is named after the same Josif Pančić, physician and botanist.[[8]](https://mto.gov.rs/)

:::fact{title="Two parks, one summer day"}
Tara and Kopaonik became national parks on the same day, 13 July 1981. Kopaonik's law was passed by the Assembly of Socialist Serbia; Tara was proclaimed on the same date.[[5]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/biljni-svet.html)[[6]](https://npkopaonik.rs/o-nama/)
:::

**Šar planina** (*Nacionalni park Šar planina*) is the fifth name in the Serbian law. It is a high mountain park in the south of Kosovo, first declared in 1986, and the law singles out its glacial landscape of cirques, moraines and mountain lakes.[[2]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Zakon-o-naconalnim-parkovima.pdf)[[9]](https://www.ammk-rks.net/sr/drejtorite/30/drejtoria-parku-kombetar-sharr) It is the one a guide from Serbia is least likely to visit with a group. Today it is run by a park directorate based in Prizren. That directorate's own history says the park's work was interrupted between 1999 and 2002 and resumed in May 2002.[[9]](https://www.ammk-rks.net/sr/drejtorite/30/drejtoria-parku-kombetar-sharr) Serbian law still lists it, while a later Kosovo law for the park expanded its territory, so the two sets of boundaries are not the same.[[10]](https://www.ammk-rks.net/en/lajmi-single/153) When a visitor asks, those plain facts are enough: the park exists, it is in Kosovo, and two legal systems describe it differently.

## The three zones every guide should know

Inside a park, not every hillside is treated alike. Serbian protected areas are divided into zones under three [protection regimes](#glossary-protection-regime), and a guide planning a route needs to know which zone the group will be walking through.

The [first-degree regime](#glossary-first-degree-regime) is the strictest. It bans the use of natural resources and any construction.[[11]](https://pzzp.rs/zastita-prirode/zastita-prirode/zone-zastite.html) It does not automatically mean "no entry", though. The government regulation on protection regimes allows controlled visits for educational, recreational and general cultural purposes, as long as they do not work against the conservation goals.[[12]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Уредбу-о-режимима-заштите.pdf) In practice that means arranging the visit with the park in advance, not walking in because the path happens to be open.

The [second-degree regime](#glossary-second-degree-regime) is active protection. Here the park can restore habitats, and traditional activities and limited use of natural resources are allowed, but only in a sustainable and strictly controlled way.[[11]](https://pzzp.rs/zastita-prirode/zastita-prirode/zone-zastite.html) The [third-degree regime](#glossary-third-degree-regime) is the most relaxed. It allows a wider range of managed activity, including the development of villages and farms.[[11]](https://pzzp.rs/zastita-prirode/zastita-prirode/zone-zastite.html) This is where villages and farms can grow.

Every park also has a [manager](#glossary-manager) (*upravljač*), the organisation that runs it, and the law gives that manager responsibility for organising visits and guided tours through its own rangers or trained guides.[[2]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Zakon-o-naconalnim-parkovima.pdf) For a working guide this has a practical side. Fruška Gora, for example, asks that all organised group visits be announced in advance.[[13]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/obavestenje-za-organizovane-posete/) Its rules for visitors forbid lighting fires outside picnic areas, driving motor vehicles off public roads, and catching or disturbing animals.[[3]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/) Details differ from park to park, so the safe habit is to check with the manager before every new route.

:::fact{title="The strictest zone is not a locked door"}
Even first-degree zones, where building and harvesting are banned, can be opened for controlled educational visits. The visit has to be arranged with the park in advance, not assumed.[[12]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Уредбу-о-режимима-заштите.pdf)
:::

## Labels on top of labels

Visitors often arrive with a different vocabulary. They have read that a place is "UNESCO", or "a Ramsar site", or "a geopark", and they may assume these are all the same thing as a national park. They are not, and Đerdap is the best place to explain why, because it carries three titles at once.

In 2020 UNESCO endorsed Đerdap as Serbia's first [UNESCO Global Geopark](#glossary-geopark).[[14]](https://npdjerdap.rs/en/the-republic-of-serbia-got-its-first-geopark/) The geopark is larger than the national park. It includes the park and other protected areas around it.[[14]](https://npdjerdap.rs/en/the-republic-of-serbia-got-its-first-geopark/) In the same year, on 8 June 2020, Đerdap was also listed as a [Ramsar site](#glossary-ramsar-site), a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.[[15]](https://rsis.ramsar.org/RISapp/files/RISrep/RS2442RIS_2012_en.pdf) Each title answers a different question, and each has its own boundary on the map.

Two more labels come up often. Tara and the Drina canyon have been on UNESCO's World Heritage Tentative List since 2002.[[16]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1698/) "Tentative" means a candidate, not a World Heritage Site, and a careful guide says so. A [biosphere reserve](#glossary-biosphere-reserve) is yet another UNESCO programme, one that links conservation with the lives of people in the area. Serbia's example is Golija–Studenica in the south-west.[[17]](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/golija-studenica) Then there are the international conservation categories of IUCN. A national park in IUCN's sense is a large natural or near-natural area protecting ecological processes, with room for visitors.[[18]](https://portals.iucn.org/library/sites/library/files/documents/pag-021.pdf) The Serbian title and the IUCN category do not always line up: in a 2021 evaluation, IUCN listed Kopaonik in its national-park category but Fruška Gora in category V, the one for protected landscapes shaped by people.[[19]](https://whc.unesco.org/archive/2021/whc21-44com-inf8B2.Add-en.pdf) For a long-settled monastery mountain, that is not a demotion. It is a fair description.

:::fact{title="A geopark is not a World Heritage Site"}
Đerdap's 2020 UNESCO title is a Global Geopark, a different programme from UNESCO World Heritage. Visitors mix them up all the time.[[14]](https://npdjerdap.rs/en/the-republic-of-serbia-got-its-first-geopark/)
:::

Serbian law also has protected areas below the rank of national park, and a guide will meet their names on signposts. A [nature park](#glossary-nature-park) (*park prirode*) protects well-preserved ecosystems and scenic landscapes.[[1]](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/srb104543consolidated.pdf) A [landscape of outstanding features](#glossary-landscape-of-outstanding-features) (*predeo izuzetnih odlika*) protects a recognisable landscape with notable natural and cultural values.[[1]](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/srb104543consolidated.pdf) A [special nature reserve](#glossary-special-nature-reserve) (*specijalni rezervat prirode*) protects unaltered or barely altered nature, and a [natural monument](#glossary-natural-monument) (*spomenik prirode*) is a smaller natural feature or place.[[1]](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/srb104543consolidated.pdf)

## Two parks in waiting

The list of five has not changed in more than forty years, but two more parks are on the way. On 5 January 2022 the procedure to protect Stara planina and Kučaj–Beljanica as national parks was formally opened.[[20]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Program-rada-za-2024.-godinu.pdf) The Environment Ministry's notices describe both as national parks in the making.[[21]](https://ekologija.gov.rs/obavestenja/zastita-prirode/obavestenje-o-postupku-pokretanja-i-promene-vrste-zastite-u-nacionalnom-parku-stara-planina-0)[[22]](https://ekologija.gov.rs/obavestenja/zastita-prirode/obavestenje-o-postupku-pokretanja-zastite-u-nacionalnom-parku-kucaj-beljanica-0)

The two are very different. Stara planina, the long border range in the east, was already protected as a nature park under a 1997 government decree, so its new status would be an upgrade.[[21]](https://ekologija.gov.rs/obavestenja/zastita-prirode/obavestenje-o-postupku-pokretanja-i-promene-vrste-zastite-u-nacionalnom-parku-stara-planina-0) Kučaj–Beljanica, in eastern Serbia between the Žagubica basin and the Velika Morava, is the largest limestone massif in Serbia.[[22]](https://ekologija.gov.rs/obavestenja/zastita-prirode/obavestenje-o-postupku-pokretanja-zastite-u-nacionalnom-parku-kucaj-beljanica-0) Opening the procedure is not the same as proclaiming a park. In September 2025 the State Audit Institution still described both as proposed new national parks.[[23]](https://www.dri.rs/storage/newaudits/2025_%D0%A1%D0%95%D0%9A%D0%A2%D0%9E%D0%A0%204_IZV_%D0%9C%D0%98%D0%9D%D0%98%D0%A1%D0%A2%D0%90%D0%A0%D0%A1%D0%A2%D0%92%D0%9E%20%D0%97%D0%90%D0%A8%D0%A2%D0%98%D0%A2%D0%95%20%D0%96%D0%98%D0%92%D0%9E%D0%A2%D0%9D%D0%95%20%D0%A1%D0%A0%D0%95%D0%94%D0%98%D0%9D%D0%95,%20%D0%91%D0%95%D0%9E%D0%93%D0%A0%D0%90%D0%94_S_B42D422B-9D52-42CD-B0A0-0E6A43E342EB.pdf) Until the final act is passed, a guide should call them proposed national parks.

:::fact{title="Proposed, not proclaimed"}
Stara planina and Kučaj–Beljanica have been under protection procedure since January 2022. In 2025 the state auditors still called them proposed national parks.[[20]](https://zzps.rs/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Program-rada-za-2024.-godinu.pdf)[[23]](https://www.dri.rs/storage/newaudits/2025_%D0%A1%D0%95%D0%9A%D0%A2%D0%9E%D0%A0%204_IZV_%D0%9C%D0%98%D0%9D%D0%98%D0%A1%D0%A2%D0%90%D0%A0%D0%A1%D0%A2%D0%92%D0%9E%20%D0%97%D0%90%D0%A8%D0%A2%D0%98%D0%A2%D0%95%20%D0%96%D0%98%D0%92%D0%9E%D0%A2%D0%9D%D0%95%20%D0%A1%D0%A0%D0%95%D0%94%D0%98%D0%9D%D0%95,%20%D0%91%D0%95%D0%9E%D0%93%D0%A0%D0%90%D0%94_S_B42D422B-9D52-42CD-B0A0-0E6A43E342EB.pdf)

## A botanist on two mountains

One name runs through this series like a trail marker. Josif Pančić, whom the botanist Olja Vasić calls the father of Serbian botany, first heard of the omorika spruce in 1856. He found it on Tara nineteen years later, in 1875.[[24]](https://herbmedit.org/storage/1018/23-209.pdf)[[5]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/biljni-svet.html) The tree carries his name, and so does the highest peak of Kopaonik.[[8]](https://mto.gov.rs/) A guide who tells that story on Tara can promise the group they will meet Pančić again, on a mountaintop.

The parts that follow take the parks one at a time: Fruška Gora's monasteries and vineyards, Đerdap's gorge and the people who lived along it, Tara's spruce and the Drina canyon, and Kopaonik's mines, peaks and ski slopes.

## Dates to remember

- 1960: Fruška Gora becomes Serbia's first national park
- 1974: Đerdap is protected as a national park
- 13 July 1981: Tara and Kopaonik become national parks on the same day
- 1986: Šar planina is first declared a national park
- 2002: Tara and the Drina canyon join UNESCO's World Heritage Tentative List
- 2020: Đerdap becomes a UNESCO Global Geopark and a Ramsar site
- 5 January 2022: protection procedures open for Stara planina and Kučaj–Beljanica
