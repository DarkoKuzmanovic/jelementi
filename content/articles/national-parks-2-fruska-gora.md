Stand at almost any viewpoint on Fruška Gora and the first thing to point out is what is missing. Around the mountain there is nothing higher: only the flat plain of Srem stretching to the horizon, with the Danube to the north and the Sava to the south.[[1]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/geografija-i-geologija/) Fruška Gora rises out of the southern Pannonian Plain like the only hill in the room. Its highest point, Crveni Čot, is just 539 metres high.[[1]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/geografija-i-geologija/) Yet for anyone travelling across Vojvodina, it fills the skyline.

Fruška Gora National Park (*Nacionalni park Fruška gora*) is the oldest national park in Serbia, founded in 1960, and the only one in Vojvodina.[[2]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/) It is the best place to show a group that a Serbian national park protects people's history as well as nature: forests and fossils on one side, monasteries, a poet and a war memorial on the other.

![A forested ridge with a rocky quarry face in the foreground and a flat plain stretching away to a hazy horizon.](articles/national-parks-2-fruska-gora/photo-1-v1.jpg "Fruška Gora's wooded slopes and the plain beyond. Photo: MareNs93, CC BY-SA 4.0")

## An island without a sea

The mountain's best story is the oldest. Millions of years ago the plain around it lay under the [Pannonian Sea](#glossary-pannonian-sea), and Fruška Gora was an island.[[3]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) That is why the view looks the way it does. The sea is gone, but the island is still standing above where the water used to be.

It is still standing for a geological reason too. The park explains that Fruška Gora is a [horst](#glossary-horst), a block of the earth's crust that has been slowly rising while most of the blocks around it sank for millions of years and were buried under younger sediments.[[1]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/geografija-i-geologija/) Those sediments kept a record of the past. In them are fossils of freshwater animals and microscopic plants, and spores and pollen grains that show how the climate changed over time.[[1]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/geografija-i-geologija/)

:::fact{title="Volcanoes under the sea"}
The rocks around Vrdnik include [tuff](#glossary-tuff), made of settled volcanic ash. According to the park, it shows that underwater volcanoes once erupted in the area.[[1]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/geografija-i-geologija/)
:::

A guide should add one rule here. Collecting fossils and minerals is forbidden in the park.[[2]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/) The story stays on the mountain.

## A forest shaped by people

Fruška Gora's forests surprise visitors who expect oak or beech. The most common tree in the park is the [silver lime](#glossary-silver-lime), a linden with leaves that are silvery underneath, followed by sessile oak, Turkey oak, beech and hornbeam.[[4]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/sumarstvo/) Most of the woodland is mixed forest rather than a single kind of tree.[[4]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/sumarstvo/)

The forest has a human history too. Over the centuries much of it was cleared for farming, and the park links the spread of silver lime to the loss of oaks and beeches in the past.[[4]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/sumarstvo/) On the cleared ground there are now meadows, pastures, fields, vineyards and orchards, while the forest has kept the higher and steeper slopes.[[5]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/biljni-svet/) That is the landscape a guide is really showing: not untouched wilderness, but a mountain that people have farmed, planted and protected for centuries.

There are still wilder corners. On the northern slopes lies Papratski do, an old beech forest in the strictest protection zone. Serbia has put it on UNESCO's Tentative List as a candidate to join the European network of ancient and primeval beech forests. It is a candidate, not yet a World Heritage Site.[[3]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) The park also notes that patches of [steppe](#glossary-steppe), dry open grassland, survive only in fragments around the mountain's edge.[[5]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/biljni-svet/)

:::fact{title="Only one native conifer"}
Of all the evergreens on Fruška Gora, only the humble common juniper is native. The park says the taller conifers were planted.[[5]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/biljni-svet/)
:::

For animals, the park lists golden jackals, wildcats, red deer and roe deer, and among its smaller mammals the European ground squirrel.[[6]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/zivotinjski-svet-2/) Its most famous bird is the eastern imperial eagle (*orao krstaš*), which researchers followed on Fruška Gora between 2002 and 2009.[[6]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/zivotinjski-svet-2/)[[7]](https://scindeks.ceon.rs/article.aspx?artid=0514-58991001005G) As with all wildlife, a guide can tell the story but should not promise a sighting.

## A mountain of monasteries

The Romans called the mountain Alma Mons, which the park translates as "fertile hill".[[8]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/istorija/) Later centuries filled it with monasteries, and they are what most groups come to see.

The park describes the Fruška Gora monasteries as a distinctive group of sacred buildings from the fifteenth to the eighteenth century. Of the 35 that were built, 16 survive.[[9]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/manastiri-2/) The park also notes that legends date them earlier, between the twelfth and fifteenth centuries, while the historical sources point to the first half of the sixteenth.[[9]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/manastiri-2/) That contrast between legend and record is worth keeping when talking to a group. The monasteries were not only places of prayer. They were also economic centres with their own estates and villages.[[8]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/istorija/)

**Krušedol** is the place to tell the story of the Branković despots of Srem. According to the church's own account, it was founded by Đorđe Branković, who became a monk under the name Maksim, and by his mother Angelina, also a nun by then.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) The church calls it the [mausoleum](#glossary-mausoleum) of the Branković despots of Srem, and also "the second Studenica", after the great royal monastery in central Serbia.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) Angelina built a separate nunnery nearby between 1512 and 1516. Its church is now the village church of Krušedol.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) The [relics](#glossary-relics) of Angelina, her husband Stefan and their sons were laid to rest in the monastery.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол)

![The white walls and bell tower of a baroque monastery church inside a courtyard, under a cloudy sky.](articles/national-parks-2-fruska-gora/photo-2-v1.jpg "Krušedol, the monastery of the Branković family. Photo: 11sasapus11, CC BY-SA 4.0")

Krušedol also shows how history kept washing over these valleys. After the [Great Migration](#glossary-great-migration) of 1690, when many Serbs moved north under Patriarch Arsenije III, the monks of Krušedol took refuge in Szentendre in Hungary and came back seven or eight years later.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) Arsenije III himself was buried in the monastery church in 1706.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) In 1716 Ottoman soldiers burned the monastery and the relics of the Brankovićs. Metropolitan Vikentije Popović of Belgrade restored the monastery in 1721.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол) Inside, look for a fragment of the original [iconostasis](#glossary-iconostasis), painted around 1512 by Wallachian masters: a [Deesis](#glossary-deesis) with the apostles.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол)

:::fact{title="A hand that survived the fire"}
When Krušedol and the Branković relics were burned in 1716, only one relic survived: the left hand of Mother Angelina, the church records.[[10]](https://www.eparhija-sumadijska.org.rs/index.php/библиотека/1243-500-година-манастира-крушедол)
:::

**Novo Hopovo** is the monastery of books. It is first mentioned in an Ottoman tax register of 1546. A tradition says the Brankovićs founded it earlier, but the diocese treats that as oral tradition, not settled fact.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/) Its church is dedicated to St Nicholas, and the names of its donors are carved in stone above the west door, a detail worth showing a group.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/) Unknown painters copied several compositions from Mount Athos onto its walls, including the Massacre of the Innocents.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/)

![A stone church with arched windows and a tall drum dome, in a monastery garden on a sunny day.](articles/national-parks-2-fruska-gora/photo-3-v1.jpg "The church of Novo Hopovo. Photo: Pudelek, CC BY-SA 4.0")

From July 1757 to November 1760, one of the monks here was Dositej Obradović.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/) Serbs remember him as the great teacher of the Enlightenment, but at Hopovo he was a young monk studying in the monastery's library, and it was in this church that he took his vows.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/) A spring in the northern wing of the monastery residence is still called Dositej's Fountain.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/) Like Krušedol, Hopovo emptied twice, during the 1690 migration and again in 1716.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/)

:::fact{title="A Russian chapter"}
In 1920 Novo Hopovo became a convent, and it took in Russian nuns who had fled after the October Revolution.[[11]](https://eparhijasremska.rs/манастир-ново-хопово/)
:::

## Memory on the ridge

The twentieth century left its own marks. During the Second World War, between 1941 and 1945, the whole of Fruška Gora was a battlefield and a place of shelter and refuge.[[8]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/istorija/) At Iriški venac, the pass on the main road over the mountain, stands the monument *Sloboda* (Freedom), raised in memory of the victims of the war.[[12]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/излетишта/) The park's visitor information centre is at the same crossroads, where the main road meets the Ridge Road along the top of the mountain.[[13]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/informativni-centar-za-posetioce/) It is the natural first stop for a group. From the monument's educational trail, a forest path leads to the Crni Čot lookout, a wooden tower with a view over Novi Sad.[[12]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/излетишта/)

There is a quieter kind of memory at Stražilovo, near Sremski Karlovci. The park's walking routes lead to Branko's grave (*Brankov grob*), the resting place of the Romantic poet Branko Radičević. A postcard in the National Library of Serbia shows his funeral at Stražilovo in 1883.[[14]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/кружне-трасе-за-пешачење/)[[15]](https://digitalna.nb.rs/view/URN:NB:RS:ND_471A26027D8CCC2E9695B0D7152FB1E7) Nearby Sremski Karlovci itself, which the park calls a cultural, spiritual and political centre of the Serbs, deserves a separate visit.[[12]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/излетишта/)

![A tall stone obelisk with a cross and an inscription, standing on stepped stone bases in a forest clearing.](articles/national-parks-2-fruska-gora/photo-4-v1.jpg "Branko Radičević's grave on Stražilovo. Photo: saskafoto, CC BY-SA 3.0")

For a last view, the park recommends Zmajevac, which looks out over Vrdnik, its tower and the flat part of Srem.[[12]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/излетишта/) It is a good place to remind the group how the day began: an island that outlived its sea, covered in forest, monasteries and vineyards.

A practical note: the park offers circular walking routes, such as one from Iriški venac to Novo Hopovo, Staro Hopovo and the Freedom Monument.[[14]](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/кружне-трасе-за-пешачење/) Routes and conditions change, so check them with the park before taking a group. Remember too that the monasteries are working religious houses, not museums.

## Dates to remember

- 1512–1516: Mother Angelina builds a nunnery near Krušedol; Krušedol's iconostasis is painted around 1512
- 1546: Novo Hopovo first appears in an Ottoman tax register
- 1690: the Great Migration; the monks of Krušedol take refuge in Szentendre
- 1706: Patriarch Arsenije III is buried at Krušedol
- 1716: Krušedol is burned; only Angelina's left hand survives
- 1757–1760: Dositej Obradović is a monk at Novo Hopovo
- 1883: Branko Radičević is buried at Stražilovo
- 1960: Fruška Gora becomes Serbia's first national park
