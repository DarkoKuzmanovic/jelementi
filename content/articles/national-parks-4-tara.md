Most groups meet Tara at the edge of a cliff. At Banjska stena, near Mitrovac, the forest suddenly ends, and below lies the green water of Lake Perućac, the canyon of the Drina and, across the river, the hills of the Osat region in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html) It is the classic photograph of the park, and the best place to begin its story.

Tara National Park (*Nacionalni park Tara*) lies in western Serbia, along the border with Bosnia and Herzegovina, and takes in the Tara and Zvijezda mountains.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) The two are separated by the gorge of the Derventa river.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) The park was declared on 13 July 1981.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html) In 2015 a new law on national parks added the neighbouring Zaovine area to it, so today's park is larger than the one founded in 1981.[[4]](https://www.nptara.rs/vesti/249-novi-zakon-zaovine-u-nacionalnom-parku-tara.html)[[5]](https://www.pzzp.rs/aktuelnosti/item/354-usvojen-novi-zakon-o-nacionalnim-parkovima.html) Its highest peak is Kozji rid, at 1,591 metres.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html)

![A gnarled pine on a rocky clifftop, with a small yellow bench, looking down on a river winding through a green canyon.](articles/national-parks-4-tara/photo-1-v1.jpg "Banjska stena, above the Drina canyon and Lake Perućac. Photo: Gzanag, CC BY-SA 4.0")

## A viewpoint with a story

Banjska stena is more than a view, and a guide can give it three stories in five minutes.

The first is in its name. The viewpoint is named after Banjsko vrelo, a spring that used to rise at its foot. The spring is gone now, drowned when Lake Perućac was created.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html)

The second is the long slope of loose stone below the cliff. This [scree](#glossary-scree), called Banjsko točilo, was used in the nineteenth century as a slide for timber. Logs were sent down it to the river, where experienced raftsmen tied them together and floated them down the Drina and the Sava all the way to Belgrade.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html)

The third story is the river itself, and it comes with a warning about names. The Drina is formed where two rivers meet, the Piva and the Tara, at Šćepan Polje.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) So there is a Tara river as well as a Tara mountain, and the famous Tara river canyon is in Montenegro, not in this park.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) Visitors mix them up all the time. A guide who sorts it out at the first stop earns the group's trust.

:::fact{title="Tara the river, Tara the mountain"}
The Drina is born where the Piva and Tara rivers meet at Šćepan Polje. The Tara river and its canyon are in Montenegro; Tara National Park is named after the mountain.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html)
:::

## The spruce that waited for a botanist

Tara's most famous resident is a tree. The Serbian spruce, also called Pančić's spruce (*Pančićeva omorika*, Latin *Picea omorika*), is the park's most iconic species: a [relict](#glossary-relict) from the warm [Tertiary](#glossary-tertiary) period that is [endemic](#glossary-endemic) to the valley of the Drina.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/)

Its story is the one to tell. Tens of millions of years ago, the park explains, the tree grew over a far wider area. When the Ice Age came, it survived only in one [refugium](#glossary-refugium), along the middle course of the Drina in western Serbia and eastern Bosnia.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html) It held on there, on steep limestone cliffs and in mixed forest with other trees, while it disappeared everywhere else.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html)

Then comes the human part. In 1856, travelling through the Užice district, the botanist Josif Pančić heard local people talk of a tree they called *omorika*, but he could not find anyone who could show it to him.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html) He had to wait nineteen years. In 1875 he finally found it at Sklopovi, near the village of Zaovine on Tara.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html) It is worth putting it the way the park does: the tree was not unknown to the people who lived beside it. It was unknown to science.

![A close-up of a spruce branch with fresh pale-green shoots, lit by the sun against a dark background.](articles/national-parks-4-tara/photo-2-v1.jpg "Fresh shoots of the Serbian spruce, Picea omorika. Photo: Marija Gajić, CC BY-SA 4.0")

After its discovery, the spruce was carried off and planted all over Europe.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html) So the tree a visitor sees in a park in Germany or Britain may well be a Serbian spruce, but its wild home is here. To recognise it, look for a straight, slender trunk and a narrow, pyramid-shaped crown.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html) The park points visitors to Bilješka stena, a viewpoint where slender spruce trees grow close by, with a view of the reservoir and the canyon.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html)

:::fact{title="A tree that also likes bogs"}
The Serbian spruce usually grows on steep limestone cliffs, but at Crveni potok it also grows on acidic peat, which the park calls extremely rare for it.[[6]](https://www.old.nptara.rs/priroda/biljni-svet/lokalni-endemiti.html)
:::

Crveni potok is worth a mention for another reason. A short educational path leads to Tepih livada, a [peat bog](#glossary-peat-bog) that can be visited only with the park's authorised staff. Walking on the peat itself is strictly forbidden.[[7]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/edukativne-staze.html)

## Bears in the forest

Tara's forests are mostly fir, beech and spruce, kept humid and sheltered by the Drina canyon.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) They are also home to the animal everyone asks about: the brown bear.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/)

For a guide, the honest story is a conservation story. The park monitors its bears with camera traps and satellite collars, and in 2025 its cameras recorded bears at all of its feeding sites.[[8]](https://nptara.rs/images/pdf/Izvetaj-za-IV-kvartal-2025---spojeno.docx.pdf) With WWF, the park has worked on bear-watching tourism designed to disturb the animals as little as possible, and its 2025 report lists bear watching among its paid services.[[9]](https://www.wwfadria.org/hr/?327190%2FNastavljene-aktivnosti-na-kreiranju-wildlife-turistike-ponude-u-Nacionalnom-parku-Tara=)[[8]](https://nptara.rs/images/pdf/Izvetaj-za-IV-kvartal-2025---spojeno.docx.pdf) Whether a group can book it on a given day is something to check with the park beforehand, and a guide should never promise a sighting.

:::fact{title="Collecting bear hair"}
On the trail to the Sokolarica viewpoint in the Rača gorge, visitors may notice barbed wire. It is not a fence: the park uses it to collect hair from passing bears for genetic analysis.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html)
:::

Zvijezda, the mountain across the Derventa, has the oldest protection in the park. Its Zvezda locality became a [strict nature reserve](#glossary-strict-nature-reserve) in 1971, ten years before the national park was declared.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/)

## Rača: the monastery of scribes

At the foot of Tara, near Bajina Bašta, the Rača monastery stands on the bank of the small Rača river, a tributary of the Drina.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/) The park describes it as a medieval monastery from the thirteenth century, a foundation of King Stefan Dragutin of the Nemanjić dynasty.[[1]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/vidikovci.html) The diocese records a foundation inscription giving the year 1276, which is best presented as the monastery's tradition.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/)

Rača's great story is about books. Upstream from the monastery, at St George's [hermitage](#glossary-hermitage) with its church called Banja, was a centre of the Serbian copying school, where monks copied manuscripts by hand.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/) A path now leads to the ruins of the hermitage's church and the monks' cells.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/)

![A sandstone monastery church with a round-arched portal, a tall dome and a bell tower, in a paved courtyard.](articles/national-parks-4-tara/photo-3-v1.jpg "Rača Monastery near Bajina Bašta. Photo: Ванилица, CC BY-SA 4.0")

The church a group sees today is not the medieval building, and the guide should say so. In the [Great Migration](#glossary-great-migration) of 1690, under Patriarch Arsenije III Čarnojević, many monks left with the people moving north. Ottoman troops then burned the monastery.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/) Rebuilding began in 1795, and the new church was finished in 1796 on the old foundations, only to be burned again in 1813.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/) Inside, the [iconostasis](#glossary-iconostasis) was made in 1840 by the painter Georgije Bakalović from Šabac, and the walls were painted by 1854.[[10]](https://eparhija-zicka.rs/manastiri3/manastir-raca/)

From the monastery, the Račana educational trail follows the river through beech woods and ends at the cascades of Lađevac, a warm spring.[[7]](https://www.nptara.rs/za-posetioce/turizam-i-rekreacija/edukativne-staze.html) It is an easy way to combine the monastery with the park.

## Water, two ways

Tara is a [karst](#glossary-karst) mountain. Rain does not stay on the limestone surface for long. It sinks in and comes out again as springs at the mountain's foot.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html)

The most charming of them is at Perućac. There the Vrelo bursts out of a karst spring, runs for 365 metres and drops into the Drina over a ten-metre waterfall. Because of its length, people call it the River Year (*Reka godina*): one metre for every day.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) You will often hear that it is the shortest river in Europe. That is a popular claim, not an official one, so tell the "one metre a day" story instead.

![A white waterfall pouring over mossy rocks in a lush green forest.](articles/national-parks-4-tara/photo-4-v1.jpg "The Vrelo, the River Year, at Perućac. Photo: TarjaMitrovic, CC BY-SA 4.0")

The other water story is human-made. The Drina is dammed at Perućac, forming Lake Perućac, and up on the mountain the Lazići dam on the Beli Rzav forms Lake Zaovine.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) The two are linked as a mountain battery, a [pumped-storage plant](#glossary-pumped-storage-plant). When electricity demand is low, water is pumped up from Perućac to Zaovine, about 600 metres higher. When demand peaks, it is let back down through the turbines.[[3]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/nacionalni-park/priroda/geografija.html) Both lakes are artificial, and both are now part of the park's summer: people swim in them, there is a floating beach near the Perućac dam, and tourist boats sail up the canyon from Perućac towards Višegrad.[[11]](https://nptara.rs/za-posetioce/opste-informacije/planirajte-posetu.html) Check the boat schedule before promising a cruise.

Near Bajina Bašta, groups will also ask about the little house on a rock in the middle of the Drina, the *Kućica na Drini*. It is one of the most photographed sights of the area, and the park itself mentions it among its famous views.[[12]](https://nptara.rs/vesti/244-izlozba-o-nacionalnom-parku-tara.html) Enjoy it as a modern landmark. Its exact history is better left out than guessed.

## Tara on the world stage

Tara is not yet a World Heritage Site, and a guide should be precise here. In 2002, Tara and the Drina canyon were placed on Serbia's Tentative List, the country's list of candidates.[[13]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1698/) In 2025 Serbia proposed two of Tara's old forests, Zvezda and the Rača gorge, as possible additions to the European network of ancient and primeval beech forests.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) Serbia has also proposed a cross-border UNESCO [biosphere reserve](#glossary-biosphere-reserve) together with Drina National Park in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[[2]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6917/) All three are proposals. That is still a good ending for a tour: a mountain that kept a tree alive through the Ice Age is now asking the world to help keep it.

## Dates to remember

- 1276: the year in Rača's foundation inscription, as recorded by the diocese
- 1690: the Great Migration; monks leave Rača and the monastery is burned
- 1796: Rača's new church is finished on the old foundations; burned again in 1813
- 1856: Josif Pančić first hears of the omorika
- 1875: Pančić finds the Serbian spruce at Sklopovi near Zaovine
- 1971: Zvezda becomes a strict nature reserve
- 13 July 1981: Tara becomes a national park
- 2002: Tara and the Drina canyon go on Serbia's World Heritage Tentative List
- 2015: Zaovine joins the park under the new law on national parks
